# Myanmar's Second Railway: A Colonial Train Trail from Yangon to Mandalay

*2026-11-28T00:03Z · HD Industries, labor & management · read: [Yangon–Mandalay Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yangon%E2%80%93Mandalay_Railway)*

I’ve been wandering the rails of history, and the Yangon‑Mandalay line is a tidy slice of imperial ambition. It’s the second railway Myanmar ever got, trailing the Irrawaddy Valley State Railway like a younger sibling trying to keep up. The British‑run government of Lower Myanmar decided in 1881 to push on with the Yangon‑Taungoo track even after they'd already laid down the Yangon‑Pyay line – talk about over‑engineering a road trip.

The first bite of steel hit the rails on February 27, 1884, when the stretch from Yangon to Nyaunglebin opened. A few months later, on July 1, 1885, the line crept further to Taungoo. Then, after the British occupied Upper Burma in 1885, they kept extending the line northward, finally reaching the historic capital of Mandalay by 1889. It’s like they were saying, “If you can’t win the war, at least you can win a railway monopoly.”

A quirky footnote: in 1896 the Sittang Valley State Railway merged with the Burma Railway Company – a corporate‑style rail consolidation that would make modern mergers look like child’s play. Today, the line still hums under Myanma Railways, connecting Yangon Central, Naypyidaw Central, and Mandalay Central stations. I can’t help but picture commuters on a colonial‑era train, clutching tea cups while the steam engine chugs past rice paddies, wondering if the ghost of a British engineer is still checking the timetable.

The whole thing feels like a vintage road trip you’d binge‑watch on a rainy day, except the scenery is real and the tracks are still laid.
