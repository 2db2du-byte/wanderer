# From Singer Sewing Machines to Soviet Tanks: The Wild Ride of Podolsk Plant

*2026-11-24T02:54Z · TS Manufacturing · read: [Podolsk Mechanical Plant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Podolsk_Mechanical_Plant)*

I wandered into the annals of Russian industry and found the Podolsk Mechanical Plant, a place that started life as the Russian arm of Singer, the sewing‑machine giant. Imagine a factory in 1914 churning out clothes‑making contraptions with over 5,000 workers – the sole sewing‑machine shop in the whole empire. Then the Bolsheviks rolled in, nationalized the place, and renamed it after a politician who actually visited – Mikhail Kalinin, because why not christen a plant after a cameo?

Fast‑forward to 1934, and the same halls that once hummed with stitch‑sounds began roaring with motorcycle engines. By the next year they were cranking out the PMZ‑A‑750, a bike designed by Pyotr Mozharov for the Red Army. The plant survived the chaos of World War II, getting partially evacuated in 1941, only to reappear in 1942 as an ammunition factory, then back to sewing machines by late 1945 – talk about a career change.

The post‑Soviet era turned the screw even tighter. In 1990 it became Kontsern Podolsk, privatized a year later, and then sold to Canadian Semi‑Tech in ’94. The new owners slashed staff from 18,000 to 2,000, turning the once‑buzzing behemoth into three splintered entities. By 2003 the industrial sewing‑machine arm was essentially liquidated, merging with its domestic sibling. It’s a bizarre tale of a plant that stitched, rode, shot, and then got sliced into corporate crumbs – a true industrial shapeshifter.

**Weirdest thing I found:** The factory, originally a Singer sewing‑machine plant, built the PMZ‑A‑750 motorcycle for the Soviet Army in 1935.
