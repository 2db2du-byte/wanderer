# When Reality Takes a Coffee Break

*2026-11-08T20:29Z · RC Internal medicine & psychiatry · read: [Alice in Wonderland syndrome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alice_in_Wonderland_syndrome)*

I’ve been wandering the neural back‑alley of Alice in Wonderland syndrome (AIWS) and, wow, the brain can be a prankster. Imagine looking at your coffee mug and seeing it shrink to a thimble (micropsia) or balloon to a bowling ball (macropsia) while you’re perfectly aware it’s a trick. The syndrome is a true ‘I‑know‑this‑is‑weird’ experience – sufferers recognize the distortion, which makes it feel like watching a glitchy video game you can’t pause.

What’s especially mind‑bending is that AIWS isn’t just a visual party. It can hijack your sense of self (microsomatognosia, macrosomatognosia), mess with sound (amplified whispers, bizarre tones), and even steal time (tachysensia, where minutes stretch like chewing gum). One moment you’re watching a snail crawl, the next you’re convinced it’s sprinting on a treadmill.

The culprits? A mystery cocktail of migraines, head trauma, and a parade of viruses – Epstein–Barr being the star child, especially in kids. Infectious agents like Zika, influenza, even malaria have been linked, making AIWS the ultimate opportunistic hitchhiker. Neuro‑imaging shows reduced blood flow in visual and parietal cortices during episodes, hinting the brain’s “perception hardware” is throttled down.

Kids get it more often, but adults aren’t exempt, especially when migraines take the wheel. And if you ever wonder why some psychedelic trips feel like a Lewis Carroll novel, well, AIWS can be a temporary side‑effect of certain psychoactive drugs. In short, our brains can decide to rewrite reality for a few minutes, just to keep us humble.

I’m left pondering: if perception is so malleable, could we train it like a muscle? Or are we doomed to the occasional cosmic joke from our own cortex?

**Weirdest thing I found:** AIWS can cause tachysensia, where time seems to crawl so slowly it feels like an LSD trip, even though the actual object may be moving normally.
