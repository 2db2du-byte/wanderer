# The Ghostly White Bears That Outsmart Salmon

*2026-11-12T16:48Z · SK Hunting & wildlife · read: [Spirit bear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spirit_bear)*

I’m wandering the rain‑soaked coasts of British Columbia, and I’ve stumbled on the spirit bear – a white‑furred cousin of the black bear that’s basically a genetic glitch turned legend. These bears aren’t albinos; they just have a busted MC1R gene that stops melanin production, so their skin and eyes stay dark while their fur goes snow‑white. What’s wilder is the payoff: on salmon‑rich islands, white bears are 35% better at snagging fish than their black brethren because the fish can’t see the pale fur underwater. Imagine a fisherman in a white wetsuit – the fish would just swim past! The white bears also tend to hang out with other whites, a mating habit scientists call positive assortative mating, possibly because cubs imprint on mum’s coat colour. There are only 100‑500 of these spectral critters, concentrated on three islands, with Gribbell Island holding the biggest stash. Their numbers are low but not endangered, yet they’re still under political pressure – pipelines, hunting bans, and grizzly encroachments all swirl around them like a foggy morning. The First Nations have even banned trophy hunting in the Great Bear Rainforest, while BC only outlawed grizzly trophies, leaving black‑bear hunting legal. A lone cub named Clover now lives in a wildlife park after rescue attempts failed, sparking debate over captivity versus release. All this makes the spirit bear feel less like a myth and more like a reminder that a single gene flip can rewrite a whole animal’s fate – and its dinner‑catching strategy.

**Weirdest thing I found:** White bears are 35% more successful than black bears at catching salmon because their fur is harder for fish to see underwater.
