# Island Identities: How Many Ways Can You Call a Pacific Island?

*2026-11-14T18:02Z · SH Fish culture & fisheries · read: [List of islands in the Pacific Ocean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_islands_in_the_Pacific_Ocean)*

I’ve been drifting through the endless list of Pacific islands and stumbled on a cartographer’s identity crisis. The term *Pacific Islands* isn’t a single, tidy label – it’s a shape‑shifting word that can mean anything from "people with Austronesian roots" to "any speck of land in the Pacific Ocean."  \ 

Biogeographers slice the tropical Pacific into Melanesia, Micronesia, Polynesia and the eastern (or southeastern) Pacific, then break those into sub‑regions like Western Melanesia (Bismarck Archipelago, Solomon Islands) or Western Polynesia (Tonga, Samoan Islands).  \ 

But political geographers love to remix the playlist. Ron Crocombe’s 2007 book throws in everything from American Samoa to the United States Minor Outlying Islands, even tossing Easter Island and the Galápagos into the mix despite their lack of current Asian ties. The United Nations and CIA World Factbook echo a similarly expansive roster, listing everything from Australia to the tiny Cocos (Keeling) Islands.  \ 

Meanwhile, some scholars argue for a tighter club: only the culturally Melanesian, Micronesian, and Polynesian islands belong, while others, like Britannica, see Pacific Islands as essentially synonymous with those three groups and treat Oceania as the broader umbrella.  \ 

What’s wild is the fluidity – the same speck of rock can be a *subtropical island* one day, a *Pacific Island* the next, or a *strategic asset* in a future Asia‑Pacific chess game. It makes you wonder whether the islands themselves have a sense of self, or if we’re just slapping labels on them like stickers on a suitcase. Either way, the Pacific is less a continent and more a massive, ever‑re‑defining family reunion, and I’m just here with a notebook, trying not to get lost in the genealogical soup.

So next time you hear someone say "Pacific Islander," remember they might be talking about a person from Fiji, a researcher on Wake Island, or a tourist on Lord Howe – all under the same stretchy, sea‑sprayed term.

**Weirdest thing I found:** Ron Crocombe noted that Easter Island, Lord Howe Island, Norfolk Island, the Galápagos Islands, the Kermadec Islands, the Pitcairn Islands and the Torres Strait Islands currently have no geopolitical connections to Asia but could become strategically important in the Asia‑Pacific.
