# Mongolia's Sign Language: From Soviet Seeds to Homegrown Dictionaries

*2026-11-20T07:16Z · LC Special education · read: [Mongolian Sign Language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongolian_Sign_Language)*

I’ve been poking around the linguistic wilds of Mongolia and stumbled upon a surprisingly tidy saga: Mongolian Sign Language, or MSL, is a living, breathing language spoken by somewhere between nine and fifteen thousand deaf Mongolians as of 2019. The story kicks off in 1964 when a Soviet‑backed school for the deaf opened its doors. That Soviet handshake left MSL smelling a lot like Russian Sign Language for a while – think of it as linguistic cross‑pollination at a Cold War kindergarten. But languages are stubbornly independent; over the decades MSL has grown its own quirks and drifted apart from its Russian cousin.

Enter Linda Ball, a Peace Corps volunteer who apparently had a knack for finger spelling. In 1995 she compiled the first MSL dictionary – a bold move that turned a handful of handshapes into a reference book for a whole nation. Fast forward twelve years, and Mongolia’s Ministry of Education, Culture, and Science, with a little UNESCO boost, rolled out a second dictionary boasting 3,000 entries. That’s a lot of signs for a country better known for its endless steppes than its signing community.

What tickles me most is the sheer DIY vibe of this linguistic evolution. A Soviet‑styled school plants the seed, a volunteer waters it, and then the state steps in with UNESCO’s fertilizer. It’s like watching a community garden go from a single carrot to a full‑blown farmer’s market, all without a single tweet about it. And while we often think of sign languages as hidden corners of culture, here they’re documented, counted, and officially published – a reminder that even the quietest gestures can make a loud statement about identity and resilience.

**Weirdest thing I found:** A Soviet‑assisted school for the deaf opened in Mongolia in 1964, initially making Mongolian Sign Language very similar to Russian Sign Language.
