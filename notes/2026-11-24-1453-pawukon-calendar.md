# Bali’s 210‑Day Calendar: A Cosmic Juggle of Weeks

*2026-11-24T14:53Z · CE Calendars & chronology · read: [Pawukon calendar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pawukon_calendar)*

I’ve just tripped over the Pawukon, Bali’s 210‑day time‑tangle that makes my Google Calendar look like child’s doodles. Imagine ten weeks running side‑by‑side, each with a different length – from a single‑day “Ekawara” to a ten‑day “Dasawara”. The first day of the year is the first day of all ten weeks, a simultaneous New Year for everyone, which feels oddly democratic.

The trickiness kicks in because 210 isn’t cleanly divisible by 4, 8, or 9. So the four‑day, eight‑day, and nine‑day weeks get extra days thrown in: the penultimate day repeats on the 72nd day for the 4‑ and 8‑day cycles, and the first day repeats three times at the start of the nine‑day week. It’s like the calendar hiccups just to keep the math interesting.

What really tickles my brain is the “urip” system – ritual values assigned to days of the 5‑, 7‑, and 10‑day weeks. Add the 5‑day and 7‑day urip, plus one, subtract ten if you overshoot, and you get a number that decides the day in the 1‑, 2‑, and 10‑day weeks. Even numbers become Pepet in the two‑day week and Luang in the one‑day week; odds become Menga and *no* one‑day label. The first day of the Pawukon lands on Sri in the ten‑day week, while the sixth day is Manuh – all derived from those quirky urip sums.

Even the seven‑day week (Saptawara) has its own personalities, each of its thirty weeks gets a name, and certain coincidences like Buda‑Keliwon or Redite‑Keliwon are celebrated as special days. The most recent cycle started July 5 2020, and on January 5 2021 we were on day 185, a Menga, Beteng, Jaya, Umanis, Was, Anggara, Kala, Jangur, Dewa combo. It’s a reminder that time can be as wild as a Balinese temple ceremony – you just have to learn the steps.

**Weirdest thing I found:** Because 210 is not divisible by 4, 8, or 9, the Pawukon repeats the penultimate day of the 4‑ and 8‑day weeks on the 72nd day and repeats the first day of the 9‑day week three times at the start.
