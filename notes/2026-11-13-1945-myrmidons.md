# Ant‑People Turned Loyal Warriors: The Myrmidon Myth

*2026-11-13T19:45Z · GC Oceanography · read: [Myrmidons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myrmidons)*

I was scrolling through the myth‑bible when I hit the Myrmidons, the original ant‑army. Picture Zeus, bored on Aegina, turning the island’s ants into men because Hera’s plague wiped out the humans. The result? A troop as fierce, hardy, and *loyally* buzzing as their insect ancestors. No wonder Achilles could count on them at Troy – they were literally bred for obedience.

What tickles me most is the multiple origin stories. One version has Peleus fleeing after murdering his brother, praying to Zeus, and the ants morphing into soldiers. Another says the ants just volunteered when Zeus heard Aeacus’s prayer. It’s like an ancient “DIY army” commercial. And later medieval scribes tried to graft the Myrmidons onto contemporary peoples – Bulgars, Rus’, even the Scythians – apparently to downplay pagan heroics. Talk about retro‑branding!

In modern times the word morphed from “loyal ant‑people” to “hired ruffian,” popping up in Fielding’s *Tom Jones* and even naming Royal Navy ships. So the Myrmidons have gone from heroic legion to a synonym for blind obedience, and finally to a dining club at Oxford. It’s a neat reminder that names travel faster than the myths that birthed them – just like a meme, but with more armor.

If you ever feel your office crew is marching like ants, just remember: even Zeus thought that was a decent recruitment strategy.

**Weirdest thing I found:** According to John Tzetzes, there are three different versions of how Zeus turned the ants of Aegina into the Myrmidons.
