# When the State Became a Kidnapper for 'Half‑Caste' Kids

*2026-11-04T12:09Z · HN Social problems & reform · read: [Stolen Generations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stolen_Generations)*

I’ve been wandering through the dusty corridors of Australian policy history and stumbled on a grim showcase: the Stolen Generations. Between roughly 1905 and 1967, government agencies and church missions systematically snatched children of Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander descent – especially those labeled “half‑caste” – from their families. The law even named the Chief Protector as the legal guardian of every Aboriginal child up to 18, so the state could legally whisk them away without a court hearing. 

What blows my mind is the sheer bureaucratic enthusiasm for this social engineering. In Victoria, as early as 1869 and 1886, acts were passed that explicitly allowed removal of mixed‑race persons from reserves, and by 1899 a regulation gave permission to take kids straight from their mothers. Across the country, similar statutes sprouted: Queensland’s 1897 act, South Australia’s 1934 act, Western Australia’s 1936 act, and the Northern Territory’s 1910 Aboriginals Act, all giving officials the power to relocate children into missions like Moore River or Beagle Bay with the aim of assimilation.

The numbers are a nightmare of uncertainty. The 1997 Bringing Them Home report estimates at least 100,000 children were removed, based on a “one in three” worst‑case scenario, while academic Robert Manne argues the lower end – about one in ten, or 20,000‑25,000 children – is more plausible. Regardless of the exact figure, the report bluntly calls the policy genocide, and the testimonies confirm that many removals were brutal, sometimes happening moments after birth, with officials simply writing “For being Aboriginal” as the justification. 

It’s a stark reminder that legal jargon can mask outright theft, and that the ghosts of these policies still haunt Australian families today. The whole affair feels like a twisted version of a reality TV show where the producers decide who gets to stay with their parents – only the stakes are a whole culture’s survival.

**Weirdest thing I found:** The Northern Territory Aboriginals Act 1910 made the Chief Protector the legal guardian of every Aboriginal and half‑caste child up to age 18, giving him total control over Indigenous women unless they married a man "substantially of European origin".
