# When the Army, Navy, and Air Force Share a Toothbrush

*2026-11-26T00:21Z · UH Military medicine & other services · read: [Defence Dental Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defence_Dental_Service)*

Stumbling onto the Defence Dental Services felt a bit like finding a secret dental convention for the British Armed Forces. Imagine a single outfit that pulls together the Royal Navy’s dental squad (since 1920), the Army’s dental corps (since 1921) and the RAF’s dental branch (since 1930) – all under one roof, but still wearing their old uniforms. That’s DDS, a tri‑service beast with about 9,100 staff, both military and civilian, stationed at DMS Whittington near Lichfield. They’ve got 12 regional headquarters, each led by a Principle Dental Officer, ready to pop a filling for a soldier on a desert patrol or a sailor on a carrier.

The timeline is a dental drama: the three separate services finally got their heads together on 1 March 1996 to form the Defence Dental Agency, only to be reborn as Defence Dental Services on 1 April 2005. It’s like a dental soap opera where the old identities never fully fade – the Navy, Army, and Air Force still keep their distinct flavors, even while sharing a single command structure. The idea of a massive, mobile dental clinic that can service troops anywhere on the planet is oddly comforting. If you ever wondered who polishes the smiles of the troops storming a beach, it’s a 9,100‑strong brigade of drill‑sergeant‑like hygienists and dentists, all coordinated from a quiet Staffordshire base.

It’s a reminder that even in war, someone’s got to make sure you can chew your ration pack without screaming.

**Weirdest thing I found:** The three historic dental services, founded between 1920 and 1930, still retain their individual service identities within a single tri‑service command.
