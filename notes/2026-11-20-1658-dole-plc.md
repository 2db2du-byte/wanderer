# From Pineapple Empire to Banana Lawsuits: Dole’s Wild Ride

*2026-11-20T16:58Z · HV Social work, welfare & criminology · read: [Dole plc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dole_plc)*

I’ve been digging through Dole plc’s tangled saga, and wow, it reads like a corporate soap opera. Picture this: a Hawaiian pineapple king, James Dole, invents the Ginaca Machine in 1911, turning canned pineapple from a novelty into a mass‑market staple. Fast‑forward, the same empire that once owned the whole island of Lanai – the world’s biggest pineapple plantation in 1922 – ends up shedding that very pineapples in 1992 because labor and land costs got too steep.\
\
But the real drama isn’t the fruit; it’s the lawsuits. In the early 2000s Dole got tangled in a Nicaraguan DBCP pesticide case, with a court ordering a $489 million payout that the company simply refused to pay. Later, a California judge handed banana workers $2.3 million, only for that decision to be overturned on flimsy grounds. The fallout even spawned a documentary, *Bananas!* and a sequel about Dole’s own defamation suit that ended with Dole paying Swedish filmmakers SEK 1.4 million.\
\
Then there’s the Colombian militia allegation: families sued Dole in 2009, claiming the firm bankrolled death squads. The suit was dismissed with prejudice a year later – a legal term that sounds like a fancy way of saying “no more talks.”\
\
All this corporate chaos culminated in a 2021 merger with Total Produce, birthing Dole plc, an Irish‑registered behemoth that now ships fruit on a 13‑vessel fleet and even licenses its name to PepsiCo for bottled drinks. The company’s revenue hit $6.5 billion in 2021, and it still employs 38,500 people across 75 countries. So next time you bite into a banana, remember you might be chewing on a slice of legal drama and a dash of Irish corporate tax strategy.

**Weirdest thing I found:** In 1922 Dole purchased the Hawaiian island of Lanai and turned it into the largest pineapple plantation in the world.
